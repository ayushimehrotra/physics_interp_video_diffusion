why it exists

On a flat floor, μ and deceleration are the same number

The earlier friction set (slide_set_v2) put a cube on a level floor, where a = μg with g fixed. Measured there: corr(μ, decel) = 0.99976, and predicting μ from the deceleration with no fitting at all gives R² = 0.995. No scene in that set can tell the two apart, so every result on it is a result about acceleration — which Joseph et al. already report as decodable from early layers.

Tilting the floor breaks the identity, because θ enters differently and is drawn independently of μ:

a = g·(sin θ − μ·cos θ)   so   μ = (sin θ − a/g) / cos θ

θ is visible — it is the orientation of the slope in the image — so an observer has both terms it needs. But acceleration alone no longer answers the question. At corr(μ, a) = −0.677 over the rendered set, a linear readout of acceleration by itself tops out at R² = 0.459 on μ. Anything above that line is reading something other than how fast the cube speeds up.

It also makes the format claim from the momentum work falsifiable on the same variable. On the flat floor μ was a linear functional of the speed trace, and direct probing tied composition. Here μ is a quotient, so the claim predicts the direct probe should now fall short of composing a decoded a with a decoded θ. Same variable, different geometry, opposite prediction.

the whole point, in two clips

Same motion, 2.3× the friction

These two scenes accelerate at the same rate — 0.1489 against 0.1509, a 1.3% difference — while their friction coefficients differ by a factor of 2.3. On a level floor this pair could not exist. And unlike the same pair in v1, the two clips no longer look alike: different launch speeds send the cubes different distances at identical acceleration, which is exactly what stops a probe from reading the answer off one frame.

The first panel is the one to read. Each θ level is its own descending line — hold the slope fixed and a falls linearly with μ, exactly as the formula says. Stack fourteen degrees of slope on top and those lines smear into a band, which is what drops the correlation from 0.9998 to 0.68 and leaves roughly half of μ's variance unreachable from acceleration.

The check that wasn't there

This set exists because its predecessor, incline_set_v1, rendered 150/150 with every check green and was still unprobeable. The validator asked whether μ is readable from frame 0. It is not: the cube's frame-0 centroid does move across the set — 11 px in x, 14 px in y — but that movement is θ (r = +0.95, −0.98), which is drawn independently of μ (r = −0.07, +0.02). The check is correct and it passes.

Nothing tests frames 1 through 48. And because START_S is pinned and every cube is released from rest, position at frame k is exactly s₀ + ½·a·(k/8)². Displacement is acceleration; θ is visible in the same frame; together they are μ. Measured from the cube's segmentation mask alone, held out, 60/40 split:

So the set does not currently impose its own bar. A μ probe scoring 0.9 here would be reading where the cube is, not how fast it is speeding up — and §20's mask-only floor would sit near 0.99, the worst leak in the project, ahead of cube_size at 99% pooling mask.

This is the frame-0 leak displaced by one frame — and the fix for that leak is what created it. Pinning s₀ removed the cue from frame 0 and, by making the start a known constant, turned every later frame into a complete readout of the acceleration.

Found the same way as the other three: by watching the clips and noticing the cube sits in different places.

The fix is to put unknowns into the initial condition until one frame is no longer enough. Each one costs an equation:

position at frame k = s₀ + v₀·t + ½·a·t² v1: s₀ fixed, v₀ = 0 → one snapshot gives a, solved + jittered start → 1 equation, 2 unknowns → 2 frames, a velocity + random launch speed → 1 equation, 3 unknowns → 3 frames, an ACCELERATION

Three unknowns means three moments in time, and three points is what it takes to see a trajectory bend. The bend is acceleration — so the smallest thing that answers the question becomes a together with θ, and nothing simpler.

Audited through the real sampler — 80k draws, zero rejections (a rejection that depends on travel depends on μ), and 20 subsamples of 150 scored the way a probe would score them:

The 3-frame row has to stay high, and does — otherwise a set could pass by being unreadable rather than by being honest. The dissociation the set exists for survives untouched.

check_frame0_leak is gone, replaced by two checks. check_single_frame_leak sweeps every frame in world coordinates — cheap, no pixels. check_mask_leak runs the same attack on the segmentation masks in pixel space, which is what the model actually sees; it is §20's mask-only floor applied before capture instead of after. Both gate at R² < 0.25 averaged over 20 repeated 60/40 splits, because one split at n = 150 is too noisy to gate on. Both were confirmed to fail this set — 0.997 and 0.975 — and to pass five independent post-fix draws at 0.008–0.066.

The sibling sets are not affected in this form — slide_set_v2 jitters its start by ±0.4 and its launch speed over 1.00–1.60, and collide_set_v1 jitters both starts by 0–0.5.

what went wrong first

Three rendering bugs, one of which would have voided the set silently

  • The camera at height 0 rendered a black wedge. Sitting exactly in the slope plane made every surface point edge-on: the slope covered half the frame at luma 0.6 of 255. At height 5.0 it is lit but fills 89% of frame and the slope/background boundary — the only cue for θ — leaves the image. 1.5 keeps both.
  • Six validation checks passed on that black render. The appearance-leak check measures the μ-driven swing in floor luma, which was correctly ~0 — on a floor that was black. It answered its own question honestly while the premise underneath was broken. Hence the visibility check above.
  • The frame-0 leak, and no check saw it. Centring each trajectory on the origin made the start position a deterministic function of travel, hence of a: corr(s₀, a) = −0.9998. Since θ is visible in frame 0 too, μ was recoverable from the first frame alone at R² = 1.0000. A probe could have scored perfectly on a model representing no dynamics whatsoever. The start is now pinned; the check reads −1.29.

This is the identity trap arriving through the rendering rather than the physics — a third distinct route into the same failure, after the algebraic one (momentum) and the correlational one (flat-floor μ).

It was found by looking at the clips, not by any validator. Publish a contact sheet of every new set and look at it before probing.

status

Where this sits, and what it is waiting on

The set is rendered, validated and ready. Nothing has been captured or probed on it yet.

  • Done: re-rendered as incline_set_v2 — 150/150 in 365 min, 2.3 GB, zero failures, all eight checks pass.
  • Two costs of the camera move, both inside their bars: the slope now covers 58.6–61.5% of frame (was 61.7–65.6%) and the smallest per-frame cube is 874 px (was 1465). The cube is the object being probed, so if object-local decodability comes back weak, that 40% area reduction is the first thing to check.
  • v1 is kept, not deleted. It is the only set in the project with a known, quantified single-frame leak, which makes it the negative control — check_mask_leak was trusted only after it failed v1 at 0.975 and passed v2 at 0.066.
  • Then: activation capture on both encoders, then μ direct-vs-composed against decoded a and θ. The prediction on record is that direct now falls short of composed, since μ became a quotient.
  • The bar to clear is 0.459, not zero — that is what acceleration alone linearly affords on μ here. On the set as rendered, the mask-only floor is closer to 0.99.
  • Siblings: collide_set_v1 (two cubes, shared floor) is at 26/150 and paused; slide_set_v2's pixels are not on this box.
  • Uncommitted: the generator, the validator and this set's contact sheet are single-copy on a machine that has already lost a cache to a rebuild once.
