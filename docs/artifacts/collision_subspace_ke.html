Collision Subspace and Reduced-Mass KE

Two probing experiments on frozen video encoders: what the representation of a collision holds before contact, how a momentum violation shows up in it, and whether kinetic energy is one quantity or two factors when only the reduced mass changes.

VideoMAEv2-giant · 40 blocksV-JEPA2-ViT-L · 24 blocksconservation_set_1000 · 997 clipske_factor_set · 1000 clips

What came out

  • Before contact both encoders hold the line of valid outcomes, not the point on it. The legal outcome decodes at R² 0.68 / 0.71 from clips that stop at contact, against a 0.93 ceiling set by restitution, which no observer can see before impact. Their decoded restitution does not track the true one until the aftermath is on screen.
  • V-JEPA2's pre-collision prediction respects the collision geometry; the giant's does not. In the last two frames before contact, V-JEPA2's errors stay on the valid line more than shuffled errors of the same size (p = 0.005 on all three statistics). The giant's only do once the outcome is visible.
  • A violation is represented as a momentum drift in the right direction, at roughly half its size. With the models' own decoded masses it separates violating twins at AUC 0.63 / 0.58 (paired p 10⁻¹³ / 10⁻⁸). The models' own pre-collision expectation barely registers it (AUC 0.54 / 0.51).
  • Kinetic energy moved only by the reduced mass reads at ceiling, but there is no shared KE direction across routes. A KE readout calibrated on speed does not follow KE when only mass changes (slope ≈ 0, energy code would give 1); calibrated on mass it follows speed at 0.17–0.24. The naive cross-family slope of ≈ 1 is exactly what decoding μ and v_rel separately and adding them gives.

Experiment 1 · §62

The valid-collision subspace, read before contact

For two smooth frictionless spheres, every legal outcome (ΔvA, ΔvB) lies on one line per scene, J·(−n/mA, +n/mB). The other three directions of outcome space are forbidden: a tangential impulse, and a common drift along or across the contact normal n. A drift is a momentum change. Each violating clip in this set is its honest twin plus a pure drift along n.

Part A decodes the legal outcome from clips that end at frame k, with a readout trained on honest clips only, and asks how much of the prediction stays on the scene's line. Part B decodes the observed outcome from full clips, still with an honest-only readout, and asks how far violating clips land off the line and in which direction.

On-line = median share of the decoded outcome's squared length lying on the legal line. Tangential = tangential impulse over total impulse. Drift = implied momentum change over transferred impulse. Each cell shows observed / shuffled; bold marks p ≤ 0.01. Twin Δ = largest relative activation difference between an honest clip and its violating twin, which must be ≈ 0 before contact.

Part B: how violating collisions deviate

Membership = distance of the outcome from its scene's legal line divided by its length. The paired test counts twin pairs where the violating clip scores higher; it is the null here, because shuffling whole groups does nothing on a balanced twin design. The decoded-outcome rows use readouts fit on honest clips only.

What this does and does not show. Before contact the encoders know which outcomes are physically possible and, correctly, not which one will happen. After contact the edited aftermath is in the representation, so an honest-trained readout plus the masses can certify the violation. Nothing here shows the model applies conservation: its own pre-collision expectation barely moves (AUC 0.54 / 0.51), consistent with the earlier V-JEPA2 surprise result, which found no reaction to impossible momentum.

Experiment 2 · §63

Kinetic energy with the reduced mass varied

Each group in ke_factor_set is one scene rendered at five kinetic energies, reached by one route. In family M both masses are scaled by c, so the reduced mass μ = mAmB/(mA+mB), the total KE and Krel = ½μvrel² all scale by c while the motion is the same video at every level. In family S both speeds are scaled instead. A model that holds KE as one quantity should read it the same way whichever route moved it.

Within-group slope: predicted on true log KE after removing each group's mean, so only the level-to-level change counts. The reduced-mass readout uses the cell chosen on family S, so family M never picks its own cell. Material alone explains 58.5% of the within-group variance in M, so holding it fixed leaves a well-posed slope.

The cross-route test

Fit a KE readout on one family and apply it unchanged to the other. The within-group slope says what the readout encodes:

Expected slope by code
DirectionEnergyMomentumSingle factor
Train on S (speed), apply to M (mass)120 (speed code)
Train on M (mass), apply to S (speed)10.50 (mass code)
The naive version is an identity. S groups sit at five mass levels and M groups at five speed levels, so each source family already contains the other route between groups. Since log Krel = log μ + 2 log vrel − log 2, any representation with decodable μ and vrel transfers at slope 1. Measured: raw transfer equals the transfer of μ and vrel decoded separately and added, to the second decimal. The real test is the route-calibrated readout, trained on group-demeaned source data so it sees one route only.
The open thread. Holding the material fixed lifts the speed-calibrated readout's response to mass to 0.42 / 0.46: material flips push it down, same-material mass increases push it up. Within one material a heavier ball is a bigger ball, so a frame-wide speed feature that grows with the area of moving pixels predicts this with no energy concept at all. This set cannot separate the two; an M family that changes mass through density alone, at fixed size, would.
