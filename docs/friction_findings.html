Friction in Video World Models

Do video encoders represent the coefficient of friction, or do they track acceleration and let the probe do the arithmetic? Across three datasets, two encoders and two probe protocols, the answer is the second one — and the decisive evidence is that a friction readout does not survive a change of floor geometry.

Models VideoMAE-base · V-JEPA2-ViT-L Datasets slide_set_v2 (300) · incline_set_v2 (150) Simulator Kubric / Bullet Probes ridge · Joseph et al. linear · attentive-MLP

A linear readout that decodes μ at 0.94 within one floor geometry transfers none of it to the other, while a visible control crosses the same gap at 0.83–0.95 — and the transferred probe predicts the target set's acceleration instead. These models hold no geometry-independent coefficient of friction.

§32

The flat floor was measuring acceleration

Friction was chosen as a second physical domain because μ belongs to a surface rather than an object, and because deceleration is mass-independent — a = μg for any mass — so size and mass become genuine free controls.

The first set delivered strong numbers: μ decoded at 0.93 / 0.97, steering worked, and causal patching localised μ to the object. All of it was an acceleration result. With g = 1.0 on a level floor, μ and the deceleration are the same number.

The general lesson, broader than the identity trap that preceded it: beware a target merely correlated with a directly visible scalar, even when it is not an exact identity. Measure corr(target, nearest visible scalar) at design time, not after.

§35b

μ is exactly what a and θ predict

Error in decoded μ is error in decoded a, amplified by 1/(g cos θ) — mean 2.740 over this set. Propagate each model's own acceleration fidelity and the observed numbers fall out.

A warning for anyone extending this

Over the sampled range μ is affine in the two factors — μ = −0.021 − 2.645·a + 1.156·θ at R² 0.9972. Ridge is linear in the labels, so the fitted weights inherit the relation:

INLP, subspace containment and the composition test are therefore vacuous for this target — they confirm by construction, at any R², whatever the model contains. The direct-vs-composed tie (0.423/0.425 and 0.779/0.776) is that algebraic fact, not an independent measurement.

On the flat floor μ is a/g, so that probe was an acceleration readout all along — and it behaves like one when moved. Consistency check built in: in the incline→slide direction μ→μ and μ→accel come out numerically identical (−0.663 vs −0.666), which they must at g = 1.0.

Two caveats, recorded rather than argued away
  • The per-domain standardisation is load-bearing. Without it everything including the control transfers at large negative R² (−13 to −49), because the raw render gap dominates. The repair is legitimate only because the control passes after it.
  • The largest |corr| anywhere on the layer grid for incline→slide μ is +0.771 / +0.731 at early blocks, not ~0. But those layers read μ near zero within the incline set itself, and give an equal μ→accel correlation (+0.769 / +0.729) — so it reads as a shared low-level kinematic direction. Do not quote it as a positive without a permutation null on the best-over-grid selection.

§8

What is not established

  • Whether the model uses μ is undecidable here. You cannot separate "computes μ" from "computes a and θ, and the probe divides" — not with more data, a bigger encoder, or a better probe.
  • The steering result passes its protocol but was measured on the flat floor, so it is an acceleration-steering result; re-derivation was never established.
  • Everything is within-distribution, on Kubric renders far outside these models' training data.
  • Two mid-size encoders only. Joseph et al. find smaller VideoMAE variants fail to show reliable emergence at all.
  • μ remains an exact function of the trajectory in every design tried. Escaping that needs a target not determined by the observable trajectory — a different project, not a fix to this one.
